Campaign elements

A plan starts with structure. Know what is decided at the campaign, ad set and ad levels, when to automate with Advantage+, and which restrictions starve the delivery system of room to optimize.

The ads manager at a glance
Study guide p. 5
Three levels
LevelWhat you plan
CampaignBuying type, objective and (optionally) budget
Ad setBudget (if not at campaign level), audience, placements, schedule
AdFormat, creative and text

Choices at one level shape the options at the next. Budget can sit at the campaign or the ad set level.

Study guide p. 5
End-to-end or single-step automation
  • End-to-end solutions (Advantage+ sales and app campaigns) automate every step. Best for advertisers with no campaign restrictions.
  • Single-step solutions automate one part (audience, creative, destination, placements, budget). Best for clients with more restrictions.
  • A manual campaign can still automate pieces, for example a manual budget and audience with Advantage+ creative and placements.
Study guide pp. 6–7
Five common restrictions

Too many constraints reduce liquidity: the system has fewer options, ads show less often, costs rise and ad sets take longer to exit learning.

LeverHow it restricts delivery
PlacementFewer placements, fewer chances to optimize
AudienceA very small audience limits who the system can find
BudgetToo small to reach about 50 conversions a week
BidA very low bid or bid cap wins fewer auctions
OptimizationA rare, low-funnel event gives fewer results to learn from
Run a liquidity check

  • Campaign management: manage, scale and optimize campaigns.

More detail
  • Feed platform: build product catalogs for ads, Marketplace and shops.
  • Measurement: track performance across the marketing mix.
  • Conversion data: connect offline and web actions.
  • Community management and messaging at scale.

Step 2 of 6

Buying and objectives

Reservation buys predictability; auction buys flexibility. Plan the bid strategy around how the client measures success, and pick the objective that matches the real business goal.

Auction or reservation
AuctionReservation
Best forChoice, efficiency, flexibilityPredictability: planned reach and frequency
ObjectivesAllAwareness and engagement
PriceChanges with market and bidFixed CPM booked in advance
DeliveryStandardStandard or sequenced

In the auction the highest total value wins: bid × estimated action rate + ad quality. A higher ad quality makes an ad more relevant and lifts its rank.

Study guide pp. 11–14
Planning reservation
  • Book awareness and engagement campaigns in advance with predictable delivery.
  • Frequency goals aren’t guaranteed, but you can simulate outcomes with different budgets, durations and frequencies.
  • Sequencing (reservation only) serves a set of ads in a chosen order to tell a story.
Study guide pp. 20–22
Bid around how the client measures success
StrategyTypePlan it when
Highest volumeSpend-basedYou want the most results for the budget, with no cost target
Highest valueSpend-basedNo cost threshold, spending the budget matters, and purchase value counts
Cost per result goalGoal-basedAverage cost must stay near a target
ROAS goalGoal-basedA specific return on ad spend is needed
Bid capManualYou know how bids map to outcomes and want to cap every bid
Study guide pp. 16–19
Six objectives
Study guide pp. 24–27
Align objectives with the real goal

Clients often name a tactic; find the actual business goal behind it, then pick the objective.

  • Awareness: performance goals Maximize reach or Maximize ad recall lift; no conversion location, event or data source; both buying types.
  • Leads: a mid-funnel objective, for example a university collecting emails for course information.
More detail
  • App promotion: consideration or conversion depending on the performance goal; needs a registered app and the SDK.
  • Sales and app promotion can be planned as automated Advantage+ campaigns.
Study guide pp. 24–27
Data sources and conversion settings
  • Pixel: website actions. SDK: app actions. Conversions API: a direct, reliable connection from the server, website or CRM, including messaging and offline events, with less reliance on cookies.
  • For each objective, plan the conversion location, conversion event and performance goal together.

Step 3 of 6

Budget

A budget controls spend the way a bid strategy controls cost. Choose campaign or ad set budgets, daily or lifetime, understand pacing, and calculate the budget a business goal needs.

Campaign budget or ad set budget?
Study guide pp. 37–38
Why Advantage+ campaign budget

In the guide’s example, $30 split evenly across 3 ad sets bought 10 conversions at $3 each. With Advantage+ campaign budget the system moved $18 to the best ad set and bought 15 conversions at $2.

With Advantage+ campaign budget you can’t use accelerated delivery or run ads on a schedule.
Study guide pp. 37–38
Daily or lifetime
  • Daily budget: an average per day over a week. On days with better opportunities the system may spend up to 75% more than the daily budget.
  • Lifetime budget: a total for the whole run; spend may vary by day but never exceeds the total. Works best with daily flexibility.
Study guide pp. 38–39
Standard or accelerated
  • Standard (default, recommended): spreads delivery evenly and keeps a steady presence.
  • Accelerated: spends as fast as possible, only with the bid cap strategy. It skips pacing, can exhaust the budget early and often buys the most expensive results.
Study guide pp. 39–41
Pacing

Bid pacing adjusts bids and auction entry to the budget and time left; budget pacing adjusts spend to how likely optimization events are within the bid strategy. Spending too fast lowers bids; too slow raises them.

See pacing in the lab
Study guide p. 40
The Wind & Wool formula

A retailer wants $100,000 in website sales a day for a 14-day offer, with a $100 average order and a $20 maximum cost per result.

sales goal=daily sales goal×duration orders=sales goalaverage order valuebudget=orders×cost per result Try other numbers in the lab
More detail

$1,400,000 ÷ $100 = 14,000 orders × $20 = $280,000 estimated minimum budget. The conversion rate and CPM in the brief aren’t needed.

Reach and frequency
Study guide pp. 46–47
The Notable formula

A food brand plans a 4-week reservation campaign: audience 1 million, 70% reach, 2 impressions per week, $3 CPM.

impressions=audience×reach×frequency×duration budget=impressions1,000×CPM

1,000,000 × 0.70 × 2 × 4 = 5,600,000 impressions → $16,800.

Study guide pp. 46–50
Planning tools
  • Reservation estimates in Ads Manager forecast reach, audience size, CPM and frequency for a budget, or the budget for a desired reach. Delivery isn’t guaranteed, but you pay only for ads delivered and can cancel.
  • Campaign Planner drafts, compares (up to 6 plans), shares and buys reservation plans. Plans can be shared only within the same business portfolio.

Step 4 of 6

Audiences and placements

Guide delivery toward the people most likely to respond without boxing it in, then let ads run wherever those people spend time.

Four ways to describe people
  • Location: city, country or community.
  • Demographics: age, gender, income, occupation and more.
  • Interests: what people like and engage with.
  • Behaviors: purchase behavior, device use and other activity.

Advantage+ audience uses AI to look beyond these selections when it’s likely to improve results.

Study guide pp. 52–54
Custom and lookalike audiences
  • Custom audiences reconnect with existing customers; engagement custom audiences use people who engaged with the business’s content.
  • Lookalike audiences find people like a source audience. Closer to 1% optimizes for similarity (smaller); closer to 10% for reach.
Study guide pp. 55–58
Avoid overlap and narrowness
  • Overlap means ad sets reach the same people. The auction avoids showing one advertiser too many ads to a person in a short time, so overlap can stop ad sets from spending their budgets.
  • Fix it with exclusions or by consolidating similar ad sets.
  • Avoid extremely narrow audiences: there isn’t enough information to find the best people.
Study guide pp. 59–61
What each placement offers
Study guide pp. 63–66
Advantage+ or manual placements

Advantage+ placements (default, recommended) find the most cost-effective opportunities across Facebook, Messenger, Instagram and Audience Network, for the lowest overall cost per optimization event. Choose manual only to accommodate certain creative, exclude placements for strategy, or for brand safety, and then pick several.

Step 5 of 6

Creative and measurement

Plan formats and creative for each placement and keep them fresh, then decide how you will measure: the hypothesis, the metrics, the reports and the tests.

Ad formats
Study guide pp. 69–70
Plan the formats
  • Slideshow ads play static images like a video and use less data: good for slow connections and older devices.
  • Advantage+ creative uses AI to show each person the version most likely to resonate, adjusting details such as brightness and text placement.
  • Use video where possible; it generally drives more brand and direct response results.
Study guide pp. 69–70
Fight creative fatigue
  • Edit the current creative or add new variations with Advantage+ creative.
  • Expand the audience to reach people who haven’t seen the ad.
  • Keep the original ad running rather than pausing it.

Step 6 of 6

Privacy, safety and review

Plan within the privacy rules that shape signals, protect the brand with suitability controls, and build ads that pass review the first time.

Four regulations to know
Study guide pp. 87–89
Impact on campaigns

Privacy rules can hide parts of the customer journey, lowering performance, raising costs and blurring measurement. Cookie-dependent methods such as multi-touch attribution are hit hardest. Plan privacy-first solutions: aggregated measurement and the Conversions API.

Study guide p. 90

Inventory filterExpanded, moderate or limited sensitivity for Facebook Feed, in-stream ads for reels, Reels, Instagram Feed and Reels, and Audience Network
Publisher and content block listsKeep ads off specific Pages, Instagram accounts and apps

Study guide pp. 92–93
Approved or rejected?
Study guide pp. 96–101
How review works

Mostly automated, with human reviewers training the system and checking some ads. Most ads are reviewed within 24 hours. An approved ad can still be rejected later, for example after unusually high negative feedback such as hides and spam reports. Rejected ads can be edited (starting a new review) or appealed.

Study guide p. 95
Prohibited and restricted
  • Prohibited: implying knowledge of personal attributes, sensational content, discrimination, unrealistic outcomes.
  • Restricted: alcohol (local laws, 18+), financial services (no requests for financial data, 18+, fees such as APR and the company address disclosed), drugs, online gambling (written permission, 18+), cryptocurrency (eligibility for trading and mining; not for education or news).

Study guide pp. 8–95
Budget formulas

Auction: daily sales goal × days = sales goal; ÷ average order = orders; × cost per result = budget.

Reservation: audience × reach × weekly frequency × weeks = impressions; ÷ 1,000 × CPM = budget.

Study guide pp. 44, 49
Common traps
  • Accelerated delivery works only with a bid cap and skips pacing.
  • Advantage+ campaign budget can’t use accelerated delivery or ad scheduling.
  • Sequencing is a reservation-only feature.
  • Overlapping audiences can stop ad sets from spending.
  • The Wind & Wool brief includes a conversion rate and CPM you don’t need.
  • An approved ad can still be rejected during delivery.

Activate and manage

Ads Manager is where campaigns are built and Business Suite is where the business, its assets and its people are managed. Know what you set at each level of a campaign, where automation helps, and how to control access, billing and brand safety.

Check your opportunity score, monitor reporting, create audiences, set up billing, connect data sources and configure advertiser settings. Ads Manager is also the door to All tools for managing, testing and selling.

Study guide p. 6
Opportunity score

A score from 0 to 100 showing how optimized campaigns, ad sets and ads are, based on how many experimentally proven recommendations you’ve applied. It updates as you act on recommendations, helps before, during and after a campaign, and doesn’t predict or guarantee results.

Study guide p. 10
Seven performance best practices
  1. Opt into automated campaigns with Advantage+.
  2. Align objectives and goals with the primary business goal.
  3. Consider your audience, combining what you know with Advantage+ audience.
  4. Optimize creative and placements: diversify, and use Advantage+ placements.
  5. Focus on signals: high-quality data.
  6. Get the most from budget and bidding with Advantage+ campaign budget.
  7. Validate results by measuring true impact.

Opportunity score recommendations cover practices 1 to 6.

Study guide p. 11
Business portfolio

A business portfolio brings the Facebook Page, Instagram account, ad accounts, catalogs and other assets together, so the business manages them and the people who use them in one place: unified management, control over access and security, easier collaboration with agencies, and one place for notifications.

  • Billing and payments: accounts, payment activity, invoices, payment methods, credit lines and legal entities. Changing payment methods needs admin access or a finance role.

Step 2 of 5

Objectives

The objective tells the delivery system what outcome matters most. Match it to the business goal and the funnel stage, then set the conversion location, event, performance goal, dataset and attribution to support it.

Choose by business goal

With auction buying all six objectives are available; with reservation only awareness and engagement.

Study guide pp. 21–23
Match the goal to the objective
Study guide pp. 21–23
Settings at the ad set level
  • Conversion location: where the action happens: website, app, Messenger, a Facebook Page and more.
  • Conversion event: the action you want, when the location needs one.
  • Performance goal: what the delivery system bids on in the auction. Every objective needs one.
  • Dataset: event data from websites, apps, stores or business chats in one place, fed by the Pixel, SDK or Conversions API.
Study guide pp. 23–24
Attribution settings
  • Standard attribution optimizes delivery on the time windows and behaviors you choose, crediting conversions from impressions, clicks or video plays.
  • Incremental attribution uses predictive models to optimize for incremental conversions: whether the ad caused the conversion.

Buying types and bidding

Auction buys in real time; reservation books reach and frequency in advance. Know how the auction picks a winner, how budgets and bid strategies work, and how to get an ad set through the learning phase.

Study guide p. 29
Reservation in practice

Reach a set number of people at a set frequency for a fixed price: ideal for large campaigns of more than 200,000 people where timing and reach matter. Only eligible advertisers that meet spend and reach minimums can use it.

  • Target frequency: an average number of weekly impressions per person; consistent delivery over 2–4 weeks.
  • Frequency cap: a maximum per person; avoids oversaturation.
More detail
  • Sequenced delivery: up to 50 ads in a series to tell a story, though the order isn’t guaranteed.
  • Reservation estimates preview reach, audience size, CPM and frequency before you book.
Study guide pp. 37–38
Reach and frequency
Study guide pp. 37–38
Total value wins

The auction picks the ad with the highest total value, which balances value for people and for advertisers:

Total value=bid×estimated action rate+ad quality
  • Bid: what you’ll pay for the outcome.
  • Estimated action rate: the probability that showing the ad leads to that outcome.
  • Ad quality: quality versus competing ads, from the quality ranking in ad relevance diagnostics.
Run an auction in the lab
Study guide p. 30
Pacing
  • Budget pacing spreads spend evenly over the campaign.
  • Bid pacing decides which auctions to enter or skip.

When optimization events are scarce and expensive the system may spend less; when cheap ones appear it may spend more.

Study guide pp. 32–33
Budget strategy
  • Advantage+ campaign budget: set at the campaign level and moved in real time to the best opportunities. Use with at least 2 ad sets.
  • Ad set budget: different budgets, bid strategies and schedules per ad set.
  • Daily budget: an average per day; spend can vary by day.
  • Lifetime budget: a total for the run; you’re never charged more than it.
More detail

In the guide’s example, $30 split evenly over 3 ad sets gave 10 conversions at $3 each; Advantage+ campaign budget gave 15 at $2.

Study guide p. 31
Budget sharing and scheduling
  • Ad set budget sharing lets each ad set share up to 20% of its daily budget with others in the campaign. Eligible advertisers get it by default.
  • With a daily budget, use budget scheduling for high-demand periods. With a lifetime budget, use ad scheduling for days and times.
  • Plan ahead: ad review can take 24 hours or longer, and the system controls when ads are delivered, not when they are seen.

The first week of an ad set
Study guide pp. 35–36
How learning works

After a new ad set or a significant edit, the system tests how to deliver it. Performance and cost fluctuate until it gets about 50 optimization events in a week.

  • Learning limited: unlikely to reach 50 events in the week after a significant edit.
  • Causes: small audience, low budget, low bid or cost control, high audience overlap, a rare optimization event, too many ads or ad sets.
More detail
  • Fixes: combine ad sets and campaigns, broaden the audience, raise budget or bid, relax restrictive bid strategies, choose a more frequent event.
Study guide pp. 35–36
Support the learning phase
  • Edit after the learning phase, and avoid unnecessary significant edits.
  • Combine similar ad sets so learnings are shared.
  • Budget for at least 50 optimization events.
  • Track delivery status and add Optimization events as a reporting column.

Step 4 of 5

Signals and audiences

Signals tell the delivery system what people want. Collect them with the right data sources, organize products in catalogs, and build audiences that guide delivery without boxing it in.

Pixel and Conversions API together
Study guide pp. 41–46
Signals, events, data sources

Signals are actions that reveal intent: a site visit, app use, an in-store purchase. Events are those actions; data sources collect and send them. They power audience building, delivery optimization and measurement.

Data sourceChannel
PixelWebsite
Facebook SDKApp
Conversions APIWebsite, app, offline, messaging

A dataset brings event data from all sources together.

Study guide pp. 40–41
Why add the Conversions API

It sends data server to server, complementing the browser-based Pixel:

  • Less dependence on browsers and ad blockers; better event matching.
  • Optimizes for actions later in the journey and improves measurement.
More detail
  • Offline events: measure store sales and build audiences from them.
  • Messaging events: optimize ads that click to Messenger for purchases.
  • App events: less dependence on the SDK and app releases.
Study guide pp. 42–43
Events and privacy
  • Standard events are predefined (initiate checkout, subscribe, in-store purchase); custom events are your own and need code. Parameters such as value and content IDs add detail; pass value to measure ROAS.
  • Custom conversions add rules to events, such as a purchase URL containing “smartphone” with value over $800, and can become a performance goal. Not available for app events.
  • Aggregated Event Measurement measures web and app events for iOS 14+ users while protecting privacy: no personal identifiers, differential privacy noise, and group-level aggregation.
Study guide pp. 44–46
Catalogs and Commerce Manager

A catalog holds item data (ID, title, description, image, price, availability, link) for dynamic ads. Steps: create or access the catalog in Commerce Manager, share events through the Pixel, SDK or Conversions API, then use it in ads: Advantage+ catalog ads, Shops, carousel and collection ads.

More detail

Add items manually, by data file, with the Pixel or from a partner platform such as Shopify. Group them into product sets of at least 20 products.

Study guide pp. 47–49
Catalog best practices
  • Use one catalog, kept up to date and matching the website.
  • Images of at least 500 × 500 pixels; correct links and categories; set up variants.
  • For Advantage+ catalog ads: use the largest product set, keep a catalog match rate above 75%, optimize for purchases or registrations, and fix product issues.
Study guide pp. 51, 53
Three ways to define an audience
Study guide pp. 55–63
Broad or narrow

Broad audiences (for example ages 18–65 in the US) let the system find people: best for reach and acquisition. Narrow audiences use custom, lookalike or detailed parameters: use them to re-engage, reach small segments or reach a niche. Audience definition and estimated daily results are estimates, not reach.

Study guide pp. 56–57
Lookalike size

Slide from 1% (most similar) to 10% (most reach) of a country’s population.

Study guide pp. 62–63
Suggestions versus controls

Advantage+ audience uses AI to reach beyond your suggestions when that’s likely to improve results. It’s on by default for new campaigns.

Suggestions (guide, don’t restrict)Controls (restrict delivery)
Age 18–65+, genderLocation, language
Detailed targetingMinimum age (18–25)
Custom and lookalike audiencesCustom audience exclusions

If a control is a business need, set it at the account level.

Study guide pp. 64–66
Sort the settings
Study guide p. 66
Find overlap

When ad sets reach the same people they compete in the same auction and costs rise. In the Audiences tab, show audience overlap for audiences of at least 10,000 accounts, and keep the overlap percentage as low as possible.

Study guide pp. 67–68
Fix overlap
  • Exclusions: exclude customers from prospecting, or use location, language and age controls.
  • Consolidation: merge audiences and budgets into a larger audience.
  • Refinement: keep them separate and make them more distinct, when they deserve different bids.

Step 5 of 5

Creative and reporting

Diversify creative for every placement and mobile screen, stay within the Advertising Standards, then read reports and run tests to learn what really works.

One creative, every placement
Study guide pp. 74–76
Diversify messages, visuals and placements

Variety reduces creative fatigue, suits different preferences and gives the delivery system more options.

  • Messaging: speak to different motivators: comfort, quality, sustainability.
  • Visuals: different people and products; user-generated, lo-fi and polished brand content; bold color and motion.
More detail
  • Placements: opt into as many placements and formats as possible.

Formats: flexible, single image or video, carousel and collection.

Study guide pp. 72–74
Advantage+ placements or manual

Advantage+ placements (default, recommended) maximize reach and let the system allocate budget. Choose manual placements to fit creative to specific placements, exclude placements for strategy, or for brand safety.

Study guide pp. 76–78
Mobile-first best practices
  • 9:16 vertical video.
  • Keep it to about 15 seconds, with branding in the first 3 seconds.
  • Use audio, with subtitles.
  • Keep text, logos and calls to action inside the safe zone of Stories and Reels.
Study guide p. 78
Asset customization or Advantage+ creative
  • Asset customization: manually tailor media, text and links per placement, when specific creative performs better somewhere and you can manage several versions.
  • Advantage+ creative: AI generates and optimizes variations for each viewer and placement, to scale creative testing.

Manual customizations take precedence for the placements you customized.

Study guide pp. 76–77
Approved or rejected?
Study guide pp. 79–82
Review and policies

Every ad is reviewed against the Advertising Standards (which include the Community Standards): approved ads run; rejected ads need changes, or you can appeal.

More detail
  • Prohibited: tobacco sales, asking about or implying personal attributes, weapons pointed at the viewer, deceptive claims.
  • Restricted: alcohol (local rules, 18+), financial services (18+, disclosures, no requests for financial data), online gambling (written authorization, 18+).
Study guide pp. 79–81
Common rejection causes
  • Creative: sexual imagery, misleading buttons, misuse of platform logos, focus on a body part or pain point.
  • Text: unclear offer, implying knowledge of personal attributes, false claims, offensive language.
  • Landing page: doesn’t match the ad, prohibited content, traps visitors, or doesn’t work.
Study guide p. 82
Metrics that matter

Pick metrics from the business goal, the objective and the performance goal, which works like the KPI.

AwarenessConsiderationConversion
Reach, frequency, estimated ad recall lift, impressionsLink clicks, CTR, landing page views, conversations startedPurchases, purchase ROAS, leads, adds to cart

Results count outcomes for your settings; cost per result is amount spent divided by results.

Study guide pp. 84–85
Ads Reporting

Customize breakdowns (time, delivery, action) and metrics, add conditional formatting, switch between pivot table, trend and bar layouts, filter and sort, schedule recurring emailed reports (admin, advertiser or analyst access) and export or share.

Study guide pp. 12–13
Analyze in six steps
  1. Check results, your KPI.
  2. Check other relevant metrics: cost per result, ROAS, reach.
  3. Check setup strategies: creative, budget, audience, placements.
  4. Evaluate results, metrics and strategies together.
  5. Compare ad set performance.
  6. Identify optimization opportunities.

Common traps
  • Reservation offers only awareness and engagement, on Facebook and Instagram, with a lifetime budget.
  • The highest bid doesn’t automatically win: total value does.
  • Audience suggestions guide; controls restrict. Unchecking “use as a suggestion” turns Advantage+ off.

  • Custom conversions don’t work for app events.
  • Ad text can’t imply that you know someone’s personal attributes.

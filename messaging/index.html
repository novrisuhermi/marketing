Step 1 of 5

Solutions and policies

Business messaging lets people talk to businesses on the apps they already use. Know the channels and tools, how WhatsApp conversations are categorized and priced, and the policies every strategy has to follow.

What business messaging is

A portfolio of solutions for meaningful one-to-one conversations with customers on the messaging apps they prefer: Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp. Personal, real-time conversations expand the customer base, build long-term relationships and drive growth at every stage of the customer journey.

Study guide p. 5
Ads that click to message

They show ads to the people most likely to engage and send whoever taps the button straight into a conversation. Three destinations: Messenger, Instagram Direct and WhatsApp.

  • Generate leads by answering people who want to learn more.
  • Enrich the experience with photos, videos, PDFs or maps.
  • Save time with automated responses and tools to organize messages.
  • Boost sales through personal chats, and share catalog or product links to close the sale.
Study guide pp. 12–13
Marketing messages

Reconnect with an audience at scale on Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp, with timely updates, promotions and personal offers in rich media.

Consent is the difference. Ads reach new audiences. Marketing messages go only to people who have opted in: they already have a relationship with the business, so the messages nurture it and push lower-funnel actions.
Study guide pp. 13–14
WhatsApp Flows

Flows build structured, interactive experiences inside the chat, such as forms, pickers and choices, instead of sending people to a website. They can sit inside marketing and utility templates or free-form service messages.

Study guide p. 14
The WhatsApp Business Platform

Unlike the WhatsApp app and the WhatsApp Business app (a free app for small businesses), the Platform is an API for medium and large businesses that want personal conversations at scale.

  • Uses: marketing, commerce, utility updates, customer service and authentication.
  • Messages run through the Cloud API; local storage can control where data rests.
  • Integrates with chatbots, agent inboxes, CRM, reservations, websites and inventory.
  • Offers a verified official business account, catalogs, auto responses, multi-language templates, template analytics and interactive buttons such as copy coupon or add to cart.
Study guide pp. 15–16
Four message categories

Business-initiated messages on the WhatsApp Business Platform fall into four categories. The category sets the price and how the business may interact with the customer.

Study guide p. 17
How a conversation opens
  • When a business sends an approved marketing, utility or authentication template, WhatsApp checks for an open conversation of the same category. If there is one, no new conversation opens; if not, a new one opens for 24 hours.
  • A service conversation opens when any non-template message is delivered and no conversation of any category is open.
  • Rates vary by category and by country or region; check the rate card for the account’s currency.
Pricing rules change over time. The guide notes that marketing, utility and authentication conversations are outside the free tier and that pricing changes take effect in 2025.

Study guide p. 17
Conversions API for business messaging

A direct connection between a business’s marketing data and the ad system. Sharing messaging events such as purchases, leads and chats from Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp helps optimize audiences, lower cost per result, measure results and unlock advanced optimization for ads that click to message.

Getting started

  • Set up a cross-functional taskforce: marketing, engineering and legal agree on which events to send and how to integrate.
  • Integrate through a partner or directly, and grant the needed permissions during embedded signup.
  • Measure with Events Manager, Ads Manager and partner dashboards.
Study guide pp. 18–19
Payments on WhatsApp

Checkout without leaving the chat: order creation → order confirmation → checkout → order update → repurchase. Customers pay by card, UPI app, net banking, digital wallet or installments.

  • More sales, more repeat purchases, a high-trust checkout and better in-app discovery.
Payments on WhatsApp are available only in Brazil and India.
Study guide p. 20
Commerce Policies and Business Tools Terms
  • Commerce Policies: no prohibited items (drugs, weapons, adult content); products meet safety standards; respect intellectual property; disclose prices, shipping and returns; provide good customer service.
  • Business Tools Terms: use the tools lawfully, respect intellectual property, follow the Advertising Standards, protect user data and take responsibility for your content.
Study guide p. 22
Advertising Standards

They prohibit harmful content and exploitation, prevent discrimination (no targeting or excluding people by race, ethnicity or gender identity) and restrict sensitive content. Automated systems and human reviewers enforce them. Extra rules cover credit, alcohol, weapons, pharmaceuticals, financial services, politics, social issues, housing and employment, and some rules vary by country.

Step 2 of 5

Goals and journeys

Start from the business outcome. Turn goals into SMART KPIs that fit each team, then work backward to design messaging journeys with clear entry points, in-thread experiences and conversion points.

Choose KPIs

Goals differ by business: some sit high in the funnel (awareness), some low (conversion). Pick KPIs that measure the goal and are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.

  • Understand the business objectives, engage stakeholders, identify key success factors, choose the right metrics, then evaluate and refine.
  • All KPIs are metrics, but not all metrics are KPIs. Prefer business outcomes such as sales over likes and comments.
Study guide pp. 26, 53
Business goals messaging serves
  • Deliver personalized value with data-driven offers.
  • Generate sales with conversational commerce.
  • Optimize media delivery with timely messages.
  • Increase portfolio participation through recommendations.
  • Discover rich data insights about the audience.
  • Reduce cost to serve by moving contacts to cheaper channels and preventing inbound queries.

Then match the goal to a campaign objective. Six objectives span awareness, consideration and conversion.

Study guide p. 29
KPIs by team

Each team has its own priorities, so tailor the KPIs. Pair each team with a KPI it owns.

Study guide pp. 27–28
Work backward from the outcome

A messaging journey is the set of steps a customer takes to reach a goal in a messaging app. Customers take them; businesses design them.

Study guide p. 34
The infinity loop

The customer journey runs from awareness through purchase to retention and back again. Every interaction is a chance for more engagement.

  • Encourage discovery with ads that click to WhatsApp.
  • Generate and qualify leads in the thread.
  • Enable conversion: make messaging the point of sale.
  • Provide service in a timely, personal way.
  • Offer utility: bill reminders, back-in-stock alerts, delivery confirmations.
  • Increase engagement with marketing messages and newsletters for opted-in customers.

Study guide pp. 38–39
Capabilities and focus
  1. Foundations: live but limited launch, manual monitoring. Focus: basic features and opt-ins.
  2. Iterating: expanded launch, basic dashboard. Focus: refine first use cases, launch new ones, build reporting.
  3. Optimizing: deeper integrations, full dashboard. Focus: customer experience and integration.
  4. Innovating: complete integration with AI and third-party apps. Focus: a smooth, personalized, efficient journey.
  5. Intelligent: always-on optimization as AI adapts. Focus: measure every journey’s efficiency.
Study guide p. 40
One example per phase
  1. A retailer starts a WhatsApp broadcast with weekly promotions.
  2. An ecommerce site sends order tracking and shipping updates on Messenger.
  3. A bank connects its CRM to WhatsApp for personal account information.
  4. A travel company gives tailored recommendations on Instagram with AI chatbots and human agents.
  5. An insurer uses multi-destination ads that click to message, with AI-guided real-time quotes.

Partners and onboarding

Successful programs are a collaboration between the client, partners and the platform team. Know who does what, how to pick the right partner and how onboarding moves from preparation to scale.

Study guide p. 8
How they work together

In a lead generation campaign the client sets goals and judges success; the solution partner provides WhatsApp Business Platform access and chatbot and template software; the agency plans the campaign, writes creative and designs the journey; the tech partner connects the CRM, tracks conversion events and builds a real-time dashboard.

Agencies often work with CMOs and CEOs, take messaging beyond one channel and use messaging data to show its full impact.

Study guide p. 47
Stakeholder mapping

Map each stakeholder’s role, influence and needs to find decision makers and influencers early. Use an influence matrix, tailor the message to each person, and identify blockers and champions. Ask who cares most, who has the final say, who supports the team and who can add context.

Study guide p. 10
Why add partners

To cover diverse needs and several roles, reach specialized expertise and capacity, allow flexible billing and support, and coordinate an ecosystem. A client with a strong in-house technical team may need no partner and can build custom solutions.

Study guide p. 45
Six things to check
  1. Industry experience in the client’s vertical.
  2. Geographic reach and knowledge of local rules and culture.
  3. Reputation and references.
  4. Capabilities and smooth integration with the existing stack.
  5. Strategic collaboration on strategy and technology.
  6. Monitoring of the implementation, with timely adjustments.
Study guide pp. 45–46
The onboarding process
  1. Identify the client’s needs: business goals and technical requirements.
  2. Evaluate potential partners.
  3. Select the best match.
  4. Develop a success plan with objectives, timelines and KPIs.
  5. Define technical integration milestones.
Study guide p. 43
Stages of onboarding
Study guide p. 49
Support and escalation
  • Keep feedback loops, shared resources and regular check-ins.
  • Escalate general issues through the Business Support portal, WhatsApp Business Platform issues to its support team, integration questions to developer support, and strategic matters to your partner manager.
  • Typical response times run 1–24 hours, longer for complex issues.
  • Must-dos: proactive communication, tailored solutions and performance monitoring.

Step 5 of 5

Measure and optimize

Measure what matters, keep testing, compare channels before scaling, and know how to spot and fix a struggling strategy.

Why measurement matters

Accurate attribution, data-driven decisions, optimization, less waste, a better customer experience and a competitive edge.

SolutionWhat it measures
Conversion LiftIncremental impact of ads on conversions and sales
Brand LiftImpact on brand awareness and perception
Attribution modelingCredit for each touchpoint on the journey
Marketing mix modelingImpact of each channel on sales and revenue
Study guide pp. 54–55
Metrics through the funnel
  • Awareness: ad recall, incremental reach.
  • Consideration: page views, content downloads.
  • Commitment: website visits, sign-ups, app events.
  • Action: action intent, conversions.

Engagement such as likes doesn’t necessarily mean success.

Study guide p. 53
A/B testing in messaging

Compare two versions to see which performs better: messages, calls to action and offers. Benefits: higher conversion rates, a better customer experience, efficiency and data-driven decisions.

Incremental or not

  • Incremental tests change a variable in small steps and measure each effect; good for fine-tuning existing features.
  • Non-incremental tests give each group a distinct level of the variable; good for entirely new concepts.
Study guide p. 57
Which test for which messaging type?

Pair each messaging type with its testing solution.

Study guide p. 59
A test-and-learn culture

Benefits: better decisions, customer experience, efficiency and competitive advantage. Practices: clear goals and metrics, agile methods, a culture of experimentation, data and analytics, cross-functional collaboration and continuous iteration.

Study guide p. 60
Compare channels
Study guide pp. 62–63
Ready to scale?

Evaluate current channel performance first, using conversion rates, cost per acquisition, return on ad spend, engagement metrics and lifetime value. Then ask whether results are consistent across channels, whether they are efficient, whether they can scale without losing performance, and whether data drives decisions.

Study guide p. 64
Spot the signals
Study guide pp. 66–67
Name it, fix it, prevent it

VivaLoja, a Brazilian retailer, faced misaligned partners, gaps in implementation and falling confidence.

  • Name it: partners were misaligned or lacked the capability to deliver.
  • Fix it: clarify roles, build capability with training and resources, and hold joint planning sessions.
  • Prevent it: reassess partners periodically, meet regularly, and share KPIs and escalation paths.
Study guide p. 68
Ways to correct course

Segmentation and personalization, conversational AI for routine questions and 24/7 support, rewards and loyalty programs, dynamic content, and clear measurement with continuous optimization.

Common traps
  • Marketing messages are not ads: they need opt-in and reach existing relationships.
  • A template opens a new conversation only if none of the same category is open.
  • Automation is fine in the window, but there must be a path to a human.
  • Opt-outs count even when made off WhatsApp.
  • The WhatsApp Business Platform is an API, not an app.
